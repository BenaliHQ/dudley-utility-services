## What we believe

**Shared ground deserves extra care.** Fiber routes tie into transmission lines and run along existing rights-of-way, so the build crosses ground that's already working for someone else. Securing new rights there takes patience and respect: owner by owner, operator by operator, for the whole length of the route. *(Restated as a belief per Jenny 8/20; the earlier version stated the fact without the conviction.)* *(Garrett, 7/30: fiber "usually tie[s] right into a transmission line"; Garrett, 7/21, the rail-line build. The fuller texture of fiber acquisition, crossings, franchise agreements, attachments, is not yet in our source material and doesn't get invented; see § What this pillar still needs.)*

**Every client deserves utility discipline.** Telecom builders, developers, and infrastructure companies hire the same land work utilities do. Whoever's building the route, they get the same standard from us: title to the requirement up front, agreements in writing, and a file that's complete at closeout. *(Restated as a belief per Jenny 8/20. The underlying fact is Garrett's, 7/21: his biggest fiber build existed "just to get fiber optic to the businesses"; the client-may-not-be-a-utility rule is his, 8/14, in voice.md.)*

**A long build rewards a record that never goes stale.** A fiber build is often a multi-year commitment, and over those years staffing plans, budget cycles, and priorities all change. Continuity lives in the record: every agreement, contact, and commitment documented in Kudu as it happens, so the build never depends on any one person's memory. *(Rebuilt per Jenny's ruling, 8/20: never suggest the same agents for a project's duration, even as design intent — continuity is carried by the record and by company-level retention. Kudu framing per Garrett, 8/14.)*

**Every crossing is a relationship the client keeps.** The owners and operators along a fiber route, landowners, railroads, road authorities, utilities, are people the client will pass again on the next build. How our agents treat them is part of what the client buys, the same customers-and-communities standard DUS carries everywhere. *(Inherited belief, Garrett and Savannah, 8/14, stated about DUS generally.)*

## What we do

DUS secures land rights for fiber optics and broadband builds: route-length rights acquisition along existing rights-of-way, survey permission, title to the client's requirement, easement and agreement negotiation, construction liaison, closeout. Land scope only. Surface rights only. The land representatives on fiber work largely came up through utility land work, because the two run together on the ground. *(Garrett, 7/30; Brent, same session: "a very similar, transferable skill set." A fiber-specific phase walk with the client's own texture is still owed; the general phase discipline inherits from the transmission pillar until then.)*

How the work removes uncertainty:

- **Momentum protected, agreement by agreement.** On a route measured in hundreds of miles, the schedule lives in the succession of agreements, so no single owner or crossing stalls the build.
- **Title done up front, confirmed to the end.** The client's stated requirement, met at the start and kept current for the life of a multi-year build. *(Savannah's model, 8/14, inherited.)*
- **Agreements put in writing, plainly.** A written offer, a plain description of the rights, a drawing of the work on that owner's property. The floor everywhere we work. *(Inherited.)*
- **Every mile recorded in Kudu.** Real-time project intelligence and visibility for risk management, which matters most on builds that outlast staffing plans and budget years. *(Kudu framing settled, Garrett's phrase, 8/14.)*

## Our differentiation

**A bench that works fiber and transmission alike.** Fiber ties right into transmission lines, and our land professionals work both. There's no separate crew to stand up for a fiber build and no learning curve on the ground the route crosses. *(Garrett, 7/30; headline clarified per Jenny 8/20.)*

**A staffing engine for route-length builds.** Staffing a multi-year, multi-state route is a capacity question, and DUS answers it structurally: recruited through Dudley Land Company's 45-year network, run on the same infrastructure, compensated to stay. *(Inherited machinery; the multi-year route shape is leadership's own experience, 7/21 and 8/14.)*

**Leadership with experience in exactly this kind of build.** Leadership has run route-length fiber, including a 700-mile build along a rail line over four years and a long-haul route between major metros. Builds like these aren't theoretical to the people running the work. *(Garrett, 7/21; Savannah, 8/14. Leadership experience, attributed as such, never implied to be DUS track record. Anonymization level of both stories is Jenny's call before public use.)*

**Privately owned, controlling its own pace.** On a multi-year build, continuity of ownership is part of what the client is buying. *(Inherited, Brent's formulation, 7/30.)*

## Our unique selling proposition

**DUS delivers certainty through endurance: fiber and broadband routes staffed by cross-trained land professionals recruited through Dudley Land Company's 45-year network, with every mile recorded in Kudu, so the build keeps its momentum and its memory for as long as the route takes.** *(Rebuilt 2026-08-20 per Jenny's ruling: no same-team-for-the-build claims in any form; endurance is carried by the record and the recruiting engine.)*

To match it, a competitor would have to cross-train a utility bench into fiber, stand up a recruiting network that took four and a half decades to build, and run a land system that keeps the record current across years.

**The honest boundary.** The fiber record is leadership's, from prior work, and it gets attributed that way. The written figure that covers this line of business is blended: 10,000+ miles of transmission, distribution, and fiber projects (the client's own SOQ). No fiber-only figure exists yet; when the client puts one in writing, it attaches here.
