## What we believe

**Distribution runs at the speed of change, and certainty means absorbing it.** The annual plan is real, and it moves. Clients carry roughly a 90-to-120-day outlook of what's coming, and then the surprises arrive: an outage pulls a line upgrade forward, one project shifts to another. Certainty on distribution is a land partner who takes the change in stride without dropping an in-service need date. *(Garrett, 7/21: "It's constantly something different. They're just throwing it at you"; the 90-to-120-day outlook and outage-driven shifts are his own description. Brent's framing, 7/30, confirmed by Garrett: distribution cares about "your ability to be nimble and think on the fly and adapt.")*

**Behind every door is a member of your community.** Distribution is driven to customer bases: it feeds the homes, businesses, and pump stations at the end of the system, which puts more community members per mile in front of our agents than any other electric work. Representing the client with integrity to the people it serves outranks everything, the schedule included, and distribution is where that belief has the most doors to prove itself on. *(Garrett, 7/30: "typically driven to customer bases"; the customers-and-communities ranking is Garrett and Savannah, 8/14, stated about DUS generally and inherited here.)*

**The work is secured by consent.** Distribution objectives read "secure land rights by voluntary consent," and that's how we work to secure every one of them. A community member treated fairly signs sooner, and on volume work, signing sooner is the schedule. Plain answers, a written offer, notice before crews arrive, and an agent who knows the area carry the same weight here they carry everywhere we work. *(RFP/SOW audit: consent-only objectives, documentary across the distribution SOWs reviewed. Treatment beliefs inherit from the transmission pillar; familiarity framing per Savannah, 8/14, never a local guarantee.)*

**Volume changes the work, never the standard.** Distribution runs faster, at higher volume, with more parcels and less money per parcel, and the agent often builds the property list straight from the engineering work order. We run it with the same posture our transmission work carries: full compliance, insurance, document discipline, and reporting that reflects the file, delivered at distribution's speed. *(RFP/SOW audit: "faster, higher volume, lower ceremony, more parcels, less money per parcel"; agents building lists from the work order. The infrastructure list is Garrett's own, 7/30: "the insurances and the cyber security and the E&O." Never framed as criticism of smaller firms; DUS differentiates by what it does.)*

**Continuity compounds on ongoing work.** Distribution is bought as ongoing work across a client's system, and the operational key is a core crew that rotates from assignment to assignment inside it, carrying what they learned about your system, your standards, and your customers into the next work order. Retention is the mechanism, and DUS's cross-training and compensation exist to produce it. *(Garrett, 7/21: "a core group of people that will continue to work for you for years and they go from project to project"; his prior-company retention figures stay off every surface. Terminology note for Jenny: his own word for this model is "programmatic work," and voice.md currently bans "program" in favor of "project" from a transmission-context ruling. Needs a ruling before the page.)*

## What we do

DUS secures land rights and runs land work for electric distribution: line relocates and upgrades, undergrounding, new extensions, substation site acquisition, encroachment resolution, vegetation-management notification, rights of entry, easement acquisition, title, construction liaison, closeout. Land scope only. Surface rights only. In the field that means right-of-way agents and land representatives; running the work, land managers. *(Work types are Garrett's own examples, 7/21 and 8/7; substation sites include fee acquisition, "we'll go buy substation sites," 8/7.)*

How the work removes uncertainty, in distribution's own shape:

- **The 90-day conversation.** We stay in constant communication with the client about what's coming in the next 90 days, so when the work order lands, the staffing already exists. *(Garrett, 7/21: "we're constantly in communication with the client to say hey, what upcoming work do you have in the next 90 days.")*
- **Fast intake from the work order.** The property list gets built from the engineering work order and current ownership, so the first knock happens quickly and lands on the right door. *(RFP/SOW audit, generalized; this is how the documented utilities run distribution, not yet Garrett's description of DUS practice — confirm with him before the page. Never a client's internal threshold published as a DUS standard.)*
- **Title done up front, confirmed to the end.** Title runs to the client's stated requirement at the start and stays confirmed through closing, at whatever depth the assignment calls for. *(Savannah's model, 8/14, inherited from the transmission pillar.)*
- **Consent-first negotiation, done to the statutory standard.** A written offer, a plain description of the rights, a drawing of the work on that owner's property. The floor everywhere we work. *(Inherited; RFP/SOW audit.)*
- **The assignment finishes, and the record shows it.** Every parcel accounted for, files complete, and the working record in Kudu, Dudley's land management system: real-time project intelligence and visibility for risk management, so status moves on dated events rather than a reconstruction. *(Kudu framing settled, Garrett's phrase, 8/14.)*

## Our differentiation

Every qualified competitor claims responsiveness. What follows is what survives the logo swap.

**A crew built for the stream.** Our land managers have done a little bit of everything, cross-trained rather than narrowly specialized. The same people can take a relocate this month and a substation site the next, and the institutional knowledge of your system stays with them instead of leaving with a specialist. *(Garrett, 7/30, his words trimmed per Jenny 8/20: "came up through the ranks" implied a seniority not every hire has.)*

**A staffing engine that's ready when the plan lands.** Utilities set their year's plan and spin the work up fast, and capacity is what they score. DUS staffs from Dudley Land Company's 45-year recruiting network and runs on the same accounting, HR, systems, and recruiting infrastructure, so scaling for a busy season draws on an engine that's proven. *(Inherited machinery; the plan-then-ramp cadence is Garrett's description of how his target clients buy, 7/21, generalized without naming anyone.)*

**Transmission-grade infrastructure at distribution speed.** DUS carries the full apparatus a major utility's procurement requires, insurance, cybersecurity, errors-and-omissions coverage, compliance and reporting discipline, and brings it to the fast-turn side of the business. The client gets the speed the work demands without giving up the standard their vendor reviews expect. *(Garrett, 7/30, on what DUS carries; positive framing only, per the standing rule.)*

**Privately owned, controlling its own pace.** Continuity of ownership across years of ongoing work, decision-makers who can be reached, and a company free to invest in its people for the long term. Culture and service carry the differentiation; ownership makes them possible. *(Brent's approved formulation, 7/30; inherited unchanged.)*

## Our unique selling proposition

**DUS delivers certainty through readiness: distribution work staffed by cross-trained land managers built to roll from work order to work order without re-staffing, recruited through Dudley Land Company's 45-year network, compensated to stay, and backed by the compliance infrastructure major utilities require.**

That's a mechanism, and here is why a competitor can't paste their name over it. Matching it would mean retraining specialists into generalists, rebuilding a compensation structure, standing up a 45-year recruiting network, and carrying transmission-grade compliance overhead on volume work.

**The honest boundary.** DUS's own utility delivery record is still being built, so this rests on structure rather than a track record we can't yet cite under the DUS name. ## The client's six criteria

- **Capacity and staffing:** the recruiting engine and cross-trained land managers, structurally in place before the busy season needs them.
- **Safety record:** gap to name, same as transmission. Field standards inherit from Dudley Land Company practice; a documented DUS record still needs gathering, and nothing gets invented.
- **Experience and references:** leadership's 35+ combined years, with distribution named in both bios in the client's own SOQ, plus Dudley Land Company's 45+ years, attributed by name. The written figure that covers this line of business is 10,000+ miles of transmission, distribution, and fiber projects (client's SOQ); no distribution-only figure exists yet.
- **Execution plan:** the phase walk, expressed in distribution's shape: work-order intake, the 90-day conversation, title to requirement, consent-first negotiation, closeout per assignment.
- **Local presence:** agents who know the project area, drawn from the nationwide network; capability anywhere in the lower 48 within each state's requirements. Primary office in Katy, shared with Dudley Land Company.
- **Compliance:** the full posture, carried onto volume work where clients don't always expect to find it.
