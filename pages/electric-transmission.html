01 · Cost and schedule

An estimate built to hold

The first thing we ask about your route is the parcel count, not the length.

Two nine-mile lines can look identical on a map, until one crosses a dozen parcels and the other crosses a few hundred. That's why we build the estimate from the ownership along the route, parcel by parcel, with a market study and per-acre valuation behind each number: a deliverable in its own right, built to hold for the life of the project.

In budget and on schedule is the standard we work to.

07 · FAQ

Things you still want to know

01 How do you build an estimate for a transmission program?

From the parcel count. We identify ownership along the route first, then build the estimate parcel by parcel, with a market study and per-acre valuation behind it, so it holds for the life of the project.

02 How do you deal with landowners who are hesitant to sign?

Condemnation preparation runs alongside voluntary negotiation rather than starting once negotiation fails, and we set the final offer knowing it has to hold up in testimony. Title work on that parcel goes deep enough to support a full interest-holder search.

Almost every easement on a transmission line still gets signed voluntarily. The parallel track exists so the one that doesn't stays one parcel instead of becoming your in-service date.

03 Can you actually staff a program this size?

Yes. We staff right-of-way agents out of a robust network of land professionals and hold licenses in 38+ states. The recruiting engine, the systems, and the back office behind your program already exist.

04 We're rebuilding an existing line. How do you handle encroachments?

We negotiate encroachments out of the right-of-way ahead of the crews, and a single rebuild can carry hundreds of them. Where old blanket easements exist, we convert them to a defined right-of-way, so the landowner knows what they can build and you get a surveyed, defensible corridor.
